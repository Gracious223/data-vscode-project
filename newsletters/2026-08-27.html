Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users — portfolio managers and analysts — operate in a world defined by these terms. As an AI PM building tools for their workflows, fluency in this vocabulary lets you ask better product questions and earn credibility in the room.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them now and buy back later at a lower price. A bearish position. AI tools that flag unusual short interest can surface early signals of analyst conviction shifts.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Institutional investors are extremely sensitive to liquidity — it shapes which markets they can access and at what scale.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net income divided by shares outstanding. The most-watched number in equity analysis. AI tools that extract EPS data from earnings calls or filings in real time are high-value for your users.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
Total fund assets minus liabilities, expressed per share. The benchmark for measuring a fund's value. Portfolio managers track NAV daily — it's the scoreboard their work is judged against.
Volatility
Statistical measure of how much an asset's returns vary over time. Often proxied by the VIX index. High volatility = higher uncertainty, which drives demand for faster, more reliable AI-assisted analysis.
Credit Spread
The yield difference between corporate bonds and a risk-free benchmark (e.g. Treasuries). Widens when markets are stressed. A key gauge of market risk appetite that analysts monitor continuously.

EU AI Act High-Risk Deadline Passes — Banks Face First Legally Binding AI Consequences

August 2, 2026 marked the first moment in history that financial institutions face binding legal consequences for non-compliant AI systems in credit scoring, insurance underwriting, AML, and fraud detection. The EU AI Act's high-risk AI deadline is now in force. Banks, insurers, and fintechs that haven't completed conformity assessments are exposed.

Why It Matters

Any AI product your firm ships that touches credit decisions, fraud detection, or underwriting is now legally regulated in the EU. Conformity assessments, explainability, and audit trails are no longer optional — they're product requirements.

FCA · UK

FCA Mills Review: Senior Managers Regime Governs AI Outcomes — Seven Recommendations, No New Rules

Published July 6, the Mills Review — the FCA's landmark assessment of AI in retail financial services — recommends no new AI-specific regulation, while confirming that Senior Managers remain accountable for AI-driven outcomes. The Review identifies four structural shifts: firm transformation, agent-led consumer journeys, market power reshaping, and amplified threats.

Why It Matters

Under SM&CR, someone at your firm is personally accountable when an AI output causes consumer harm. That accountability must trace back through the product you build — explainability and auditability are now a senior manager's problem, not just an engineering preference.
