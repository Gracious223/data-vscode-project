Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's six terms cover the vocabulary your portfolio managers use when they talk about valuation, risk, and investment style — concepts that surface constantly in AI research tools and investment decision workflows.

Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net income divided by shares outstanding. The core per-share profitability metric. AI earnings call tools extract EPS beats and misses across thousands of companies simultaneously — a task that previously required hours of analyst time.
Yield Curve
A chart of interest rates across maturities (e.g. 2-year to 30-year Treasuries). An inverted curve (short rates above long rates) historically signals recession risk. Macro AI models parse yield curve shifts as leading signals for portfolio positioning.
Beta
How much a stock moves relative to the broader market. A beta of 1.5 means 50% more volatility than the index. Portfolio construction AI tools dynamically adjust a portfolio's aggregate beta to match a manager's risk appetite.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a period. Maximum drawdown (MaxDD) is a key risk metric. AI risk tools are designed to detect drawdown risk before it materialises, not just measure it after.
Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E)
Stock price divided by earnings per share. The most common valuation multiple. AI screening tools scan thousands of securities by P/E in seconds to surface valuation outliers — work analysts used to do manually across spreadsheets.
Systematic vs. Discretionary
Systematic investing uses rules, models, and quantitative signals. Discretionary relies on human judgment. AI is blurring the line — many "discretionary" buy-side firms now layer systematic AI signals alongside portfolio manager decisions, creating hybrid processes.

White House Finalises AI Model Evaluation Framework — Then Refuses to Publish It

On August 4, the White House finalised a voluntary AI safety testing framework with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Nvidia — then declined to release it publicly. The framework covers only closed-source frontier models. Benchmarks and coverage thresholds are classified. The 30-day pre-release review cycle is now mandatory for the labs whose models you deploy.

Why It Matters

Every closed-source model your product depends on — Claude, GPT, Gemini — now faces a mandatory pre-release review cycle. Model releases may get slower and less predictable. If your product roadmap depends on frontier model releases, that uncertainty is now a product risk you need to account for.
