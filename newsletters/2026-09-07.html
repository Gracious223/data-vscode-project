Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms that come up constantly in the conversations your users are having — and that you need to use fluently to build credibly for them.

Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01%. Used everywhere in fixed income and fees. "The Fed cut by 25bps" means a 0.25% rate reduction. Never say "percentage points" when working with PMs — say bps.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a fund manages on behalf of clients. A hedge fund with $10bn AUM manages $10bn. AUM drives fee revenue — larger AUM funds spend more on tools that generate alpha at scale.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value. A 20% drawdown means the portfolio fell 20% from its highest point. Risk-averse PMs obsess over max drawdown — it is a key measure of downside risk.
Sharpe Ratio
Return earned per unit of risk taken. Higher is better. A ratio above 1 is considered good; above 2 is excellent. AI tools that claim to improve returns without referencing Sharpe Ratio are missing the point.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. US large-cap equities are highly liquid; private credit is not. AI tools for illiquid markets face different data challenges than those for public equities.
Short Selling
Borrowing a security and selling it, hoping to buy it back cheaper. Profit = the price decline. Shorts are high-risk and time-sensitive — AI tools that surface negative catalysts faster have clear short-side value.
