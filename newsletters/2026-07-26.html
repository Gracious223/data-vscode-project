Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users make decisions shaped by these concepts every day. Build fluency here and you'll stop translating in meetings — you'll be part of the conversation from the first sentence.

Sharpe Ratio
Return minus the risk-free rate, divided by standard deviation. A higher Sharpe means better risk-adjusted performance. AI tools that optimise for Sharpe — not just raw return — are the ones PMs actually trust.
Yield Curve
A graph plotting bond yields against maturity. A normal curve slopes upward (longer = higher yield). An inverted curve — short rates above long — has historically preceded recessions. Macro analysts watch this daily.
Leverage
Using borrowed capital to amplify returns — and losses. A 2x leveraged fund moves twice the market. Risk tools must model leveraged positions carefully; AI stress-testing leverage scenarios is a high-value use case.
Liquidity
How quickly an asset can be sold without moving its price. Cash is most liquid; private equity least. In a crisis, illiquid assets become a liability. AI tools that flag liquidity risk in portfolios earn immediate trust.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net income divided by shares outstanding. A core valuation input — analysts forecast EPS and compare it to actuals at earnings season. AI that helps model EPS scenarios faster than a spreadsheet is immediately useful.
Sector Rotation
Shifting capital between market sectors as the economic cycle turns — from growth to defensive, or from energy to tech. AI tools that detect rotation signals in fund flows and price data can help analysts act before consensus.

FCA's Landmark Mills Review: Seven Recommendations to Reshape AI in Finance

Published July 6, the FCA's 147-page Mills Review makes seven recommendations — including "enabling the foundations for agentic finance" and building an "AI-enabled agentic supervisory model." The existing regulatory framework (Consumer Duty, SM&CR, operational resilience) stays intact, but the FCA explicitly acknowledges all three face growing pressure as AI autonomy increases.

Why It Matters

The regulator is now explicitly planning for agentic finance as a structural reality. If you're building autonomous AI for analysts, this report is the regulatory map for your product's compliance requirements over the next four years.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA Mills Review executive summary — the seven recommendations are directly relevant to your product roadmap
  • Under SM&CR, trace the accountability chain: if your AI tool surfaces a flawed recommendation that leads to a loss, who is the named senior manager responsible?
  • Research "agentic finance" as defined in the Mills Review — it has a specific technical meaning that differs from "AI in finance"

FTC · US

FTC: Steering AI Outputs Away From User Intent May Violate Federal Law

The FTC's proposed policy statement puts companies on notice: steering AI outputs toward undisclosed objectives — rather than what users reasonably expect — may violate Section 5 of the FTC Act. Hallucinations are exempted; deliberate suppression of accuracy is not. Public comment period closes July 31.

Why It Matters

This lands directly in your product's legal risk surface. Any AI output your analysts act on that has been tuned or filtered without disclosure is a potential FTC violation — model transparency is now a compliance requirement, not a nice-to-have.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FTC proposed policy statement — it is short and very clearly written; a must-read for AI PMs
  • Map what "undisclosed objectives" could mean in your product: vendor fine-tuning, output filtering, safety guardrails — all need documentation
  • Research the difference between hallucination (technical failure) and steering (intentional suppression) — the FTC's distinction is your product's safe harbour
