Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms from the vocabulary your portfolio managers and analysts use daily. Build fluency here and you will hold your own in investment team conversations — and design better products for them.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × total shares outstanding. The shorthand for a company's size. "Large-cap" (>$10B), "mid-cap" ($2–10B), "small-cap" (<$2B). Determines which index a stock enters — and which funds must hold it.
Yield Curve
Plots bond yields across maturities (e.g. 2yr vs 10yr). A normal curve slopes up. An inverted curve (short rates > long rates) has historically preceded recessions. Analysts watch it daily as a macro signal.
Beta
Measures a stock's sensitivity to market moves. Beta of 1.5 means the stock moves 1.5% for every 1% market move. High-beta = more volatile. Portfolio managers use beta to control overall risk exposure.
Drawdown
Peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a period. A 20% drawdown means the portfolio fell 20% from its high. Max drawdown is a key risk metric — it tells you the worst loss an investor would have experienced.
Basis Points (bps)
1 basis point = 0.01%. Used to express interest rate and spread changes precisely. "The Fed cut 25bps" = a 0.25% rate cut. Analysts use bps constantly — "it moved 50 bps" means 0.5%. Know this one cold.
Price-to-Book (P/B)
Share price divided by book value per share (net assets on the balance sheet). A P/B below 1 implies the market values the firm below its assets — often a value signal. Banks and financials are especially analysed on P/B.

EU AI Act · Europe

EU AI Act August Deadline Passed — Financial Firms' High-Risk AI Must Now Fully Comply

August 2, 2026 was the hard deadline: all newly deployed high-risk AI in financial services — credit scoring, insurance underwriting, risk assessment of individuals — must now meet full EU AI Act obligations. That means risk management systems, data governance, bias testing, technical documentation, human oversight, and a conformity assessment. Fines reach €35M or 7% of global revenue.

Why It Matters

Deployers cannot outsource compliance to the AI vendor — your firm is responsible for what its AI does, regardless of who built the model. If your product touches credit, risk, or financial standing of individuals, this applies to you now.

Research & Think About
  • Read Finextra's "EU AI Act August 2026 Deadline" post — clearest practical guide available
  • Understand Annex III of the EU AI Act — it lists every AI application classified as high-risk; check if any of your use cases appear there
  • What does "conformity assessment" mean in practice? Talk to your legal and compliance team before shipping a new AI feature
