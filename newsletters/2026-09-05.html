Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use every day — understanding these will help you build products that match how they actually think about risk, value, and capital.

Short Selling
Borrowing a security to sell it, hoping to buy it back cheaper later. Analysts on the short side are hunting for overvaluation. AI is increasingly used to surface short signals from alternative data.
Beta
A stock's sensitivity to market moves. Beta of 1 = moves with the market; above 1 = more volatile; below 1 = more defensive. PMs manage portfolio beta as a risk lever, not just individual stock picks.
Yield Curve
A plot of bond yields across maturities. A normal curve slopes upward; an inverted curve (short yields higher than long) has historically preceded recessions. Macro analysts watch it daily.
Credit Spread
The yield gap between a corporate bond and a risk-free government bond of the same maturity. Tight spreads signal confidence; wide spreads signal stress. AI now flags spread anomalies in real time.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
Total market value of assets a fund manages on behalf of clients. A fund's AUM determines its fee income and shapes what size positions it can practically take without moving markets.
Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio
A stock's market price relative to its book value (assets minus liabilities). A low P/B can signal undervaluation; a high P/B reflects expected growth. Often used by value-oriented analysts.
