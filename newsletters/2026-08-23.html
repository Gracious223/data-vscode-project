Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's six terms cover the core valuation and market mechanics that appear daily in the research your analysts read and the decisions your portfolio managers make. These are the concepts that will let you hold your own in any investment team meeting.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding. The market's live estimate of a company's total equity value. "Large-cap" (>$10B), "mid-cap" ($2–10B), "small-cap" (<$2B) — these buckets determine which analysts cover a stock and which indices it enters.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E)
Share price ÷ earnings per share. A valuation ratio: how much investors pay for each £1 of profit. A high P/E signals growth expectations; a low P/E may mean undervaluation or declining confidence. Core to any investment thesis.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares, selling them, then buying them back (ideally cheaper) to pocket the difference. Short sellers bet on price declines. AI is being used to identify short opportunities at speed — and to detect coordinated short attacks on holdings.
Earnings Season
The quarterly period when listed companies report financial results. Typically January, April, July, October. Analysts are at peak workload during earnings season — understanding this cadence is essential context for when your product needs to perform.
Sector Rotation
The flow of capital from one market sector to another as economic conditions change. E.g. rotating from growth (tech) to value (energy) as rates rise. AI tools that identify rotation signals early are among the highest-demand applications for macro-oriented portfolio managers.
Bid-Ask Spread
The difference between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest price a seller will accept (ask). A wide spread signals illiquidity. Institutional traders minimise market impact by executing large orders carefully within the spread — AI assists at scale.

EU AI Act's August 2 High-Risk Deadline Passed — Financial Services Now Fully In Scope

From August 2, 2026, all newly deployed high-risk AI systems must meet full EU AI Act obligations: risk management, data governance, bias testing, technical documentation, human oversight, and conformity assessment before deployment. Credit scoring, insurance pricing, and financial standing assessment are squarely in scope. Fines reach €35M or 7% of global revenue.

Why It Matters

The deadline has passed. Any AI product your firm deploys that touches credit, insurance, or financial assessment must now comply — or face fines that dwarf GDPR. Explainability, bias testing, and human oversight are not features — they are legal requirements.
