Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users — portfolio managers and analysts — operate across a complex market structure every day. These six concepts sit at the intersection of how markets function and where AI is now reshaping them most directly.

AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of investments a fund or manager oversees. AUM determines fee income, mandates, and scale. A "$1B fund" manages $1B in AUM — the metric your users live and die by.
Long/Short Equity
A classic hedge fund strategy: go long (buy) stocks expected to rise, short (borrow and sell) stocks expected to fall. AI is transforming both legs — screening for longs and identifying short candidates.
Order Flow
The stream of buy and sell orders flowing through a market. Analysing order flow reveals institutional intent before price moves. AI models trained on order flow data can detect positioning shifts in real time.
Stress Testing
Simulating extreme but plausible scenarios (market crash, rate spike, liquidity freeze) to assess how a portfolio would perform. Regulators require it; AI now runs thousands of scenarios in seconds.
Prime Brokerage
The bundle of services investment banks provide to hedge funds: custody, securities lending, clearing, and leverage. Prime brokers are increasingly AI-enabling the services they offer to clients.
Convexity
How a bond's price response to interest rate changes accelerates non-linearly. High convexity = larger price gains when rates fall, smaller losses when they rise. Critical for fixed income portfolio construction.

Bank of England Calls for AI Kill Switches as Autonomous Trading Agents Proliferate

BoE Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden warned at the ECB Forum in Sintra that agentic AI could trigger systemic market meltdowns — if multiple autonomous agents respond identically to the same prompt under stress. She urged regulators to design circuit-breaker "kill switch" mechanisms analogous to existing market-wide trading halts, and said existing frameworks "were not built to contemplate autonomous agents."

Why It Matters

Every autonomous AI system you build needs an off-switch your compliance team controls. Breeden's warning makes "human override" a design requirement, not a nice-to-have — and that constraint shapes your product architecture from day one.

Research & Think About
  • Research "correlated AI failure" — what happens when many autonomous systems trained on similar data all make the same error simultaneously?
  • Define what a "kill switch" looks like in your product: can a compliance officer pause any AI action instantly, with full audit trail?

FCA · UK

FCA Mills Review: 147 Pages, 7 Recommendations — and No New AI Rules

The FCA published its landmark Mills Review on July 6 — a 147-page report projecting four systemic AI-driven shifts in retail financial services through 2030: transformation of firm operations, evolution of consumer journeys, reshaping of market competition, and amplification of fraud and cyber risk. The FCA's position: no AI-specific rules, principles-based Consumer Duty remains the anchor, and 1 in 5 UK adults is already open to AI making financial decisions for them.

Why It Matters

Consumer Duty and SM&CR still govern every AI output touching a retail customer. The FCA is saying: we won't write rules for AI — but your existing obligations apply fully, and we will test them. That's actually a harder environment to build in than explicit rules.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA Mills Review executive summary (fca.org.uk) — map each of the 7 recommendations to your product roadmap
  • Understand Consumer Duty's "good outcome" standard: if your AI tool recommends a poor investment strategy, who bears accountability?
  • Note: Recommendation 7 proposes a free public-interest AI financial capability service — potential future integration or distribution channel
