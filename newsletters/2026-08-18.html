Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six concepts your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — and that you need to understand to build credible products for them.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares and selling them, expecting to buy them back cheaper later and pocket the difference. AI tools are increasingly used to scan for short signals — but regulatory scrutiny of short-selling strategies is intensifying.
Market Capitalisation
Total market value of a company's outstanding shares (share price × shares in issue). Used to classify companies: large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap. A key filter in any screener your analysts use to narrow the investable universe.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net profit divided by shares outstanding — the single most-watched metric in equity research. AI tools now predict EPS surprises by processing alternative data (web traffic, satellite, credit card data) before official results.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to changes in interest rates, measured in years. A 10-year duration means a 1% rate rise causes roughly a 10% price drop. Critical for portfolio managers running fixed income — and for AI tools modelling rate risk.
Dark Pools
Private trading venues where large institutional orders are executed away from public exchanges, minimising market impact. AI-driven order routing now determines when to use dark pools vs. lit markets to reduce execution costs.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a period. A key risk metric — portfolio managers care about maximum drawdown as much as returns. AI risk systems monitor drawdown in real time and alert on threshold breaches.

FCA · Principles

FCA Flags Bias, Concentration Risk, and Third-Party Dependency as "Live Issues" in AI

FCA CEO Nikhil Rathi's "Rethinking Regulation for the Age of AI" speech identifies three systemic risks the regulator is actively watching: model bias at scale, market concentration among a small number of AI providers, and firms' over-reliance on third-party AI systems they do not control.

Why It Matters

If your product relies on a single model provider, concentration risk is your regulator's concern too — not just your engineering team's. The FCA is naming this explicitly for the first time.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's "Rethinking Regulation for the Age of AI" speech in full — it is unusually candid about where the risks are
  • Understand what "model-agnostic" architecture means from a regulatory risk perspective, not just a product strategy one
  • Ask your risk team: what is the firm's single-point-of-failure if your primary AI provider goes down or changes pricing?
