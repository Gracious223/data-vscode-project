U.S. Treasury Publishes AI Lexicon and 230-Point Risk Framework for Finance

The Treasury released a shared AI Lexicon and a Financial Services AI Risk Management Framework, developed with input from over 100 financial institutions and government agencies. The framework adapts NIST's AI RMF to the financial sector, covering 230 control objectives across governance, data, model development, validation, monitoring, third-party risk, and consumer protection.

Why It Matters

This framework is the closest thing to an official playbook for deploying AI safely in finance. If your product touches credit decisions, fraud detection, or any automated output analysts act on, these 230 controls are your design constraints — not just a compliance checklist.

  • Which of the 230 control objectives would your product fail today? That's your backlog.
  • Learn the difference between model validation and model monitoring — both appear in the framework and mean different things

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Public markets run on shared vocabulary. The faster you absorb it, the more credible you become when working alongside your users. Today's six terms cover the metrics, mechanics, and benchmarks that shape how portfolio managers evaluate performance and risk.

Spread
The difference between bid and ask prices in equity markets, or between a bond's yield and a risk-free rate. Wider spreads signal higher risk or lower liquidity — a critical signal your product may surface.
Benchmark
A reference index against which performance is measured — e.g. S&P 500 for US equities, MSCI World for global. AI tools are evaluated by whether they help PMs beat or track their benchmark more efficiently.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of investments a fund or firm manages on behalf of clients. Larger AUM means more complexity, more data — and more potential value for AI that scales with portfolio size.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
Per-share value of a fund: total assets minus liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. Fund managers track NAV daily. AI tools that model or predict NAV movement are high-value — and high-risk if wrong.
Yield Curve
A graph of bond yields at different maturities. A normal curve slopes upward; an inverted curve (short rates above long) often signals recession. Fixed-income analysts use it constantly — your product should understand it too.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a given period. A key risk metric: a 20% drawdown requires a 25% gain just to recover. AI tools that flag drawdown risk early are among the most valued by portfolio managers.

EU AI Act High-Risk Deadline Has Arrived — Finance Firms Face €15M Penalties

As of August 2, 2026, EU AI Act obligations for high-risk AI systems are fully enforceable. Credit scoring, fraud detection, and automated financial decisions are explicitly in scope — regardless of whether AI makes the final call or merely supports a human. Penalties reach €15M or 3% of global turnover.

Why It Matters

This isn't future risk — it's live. Any AI product used in credit, fraud, or financial access decisions in the EU needs documentation, bias monitoring, and audit rights baked in. If you're building for European clients, this shapes your PRD now.

Research & Think About

SEC AI Scrutiny Goes Operational: Six "AI Washing" Cases and an AI Task Force

The SEC has no AI-specific rule — but it has filed six AI washing enforcement cases alleging $44M+ in fraud, embedded an AI Task Force across examination categories, and flagged AI as a 2026 Exam Priority. The enforcement mechanism is operational; the rulebook just hasn't been published.
