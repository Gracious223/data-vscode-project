Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your analysts and portfolio managers live inside these concepts daily. Knowing them precisely — not just roughly — is what earns you credibility in the room when product decisions intersect with market mechanics.

Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Highly liquid assets (large-cap equities) have many buyers and sellers. Illiquid assets (private credit, small-caps) are harder to exit quickly.
Volatility & VIX
Volatility measures how much an asset's price swings. The VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) tracks expected S&P 500 volatility over 30 days — often called the "fear gauge." High VIX = market uncertainty.
Yield Curve
A chart of interest rates on bonds of different maturities. Normally upward-sloping. An inverted curve — short rates higher than long — has historically preceded recessions. Analysts watch it closely.
Duration
Measures how sensitive a bond's price is to interest rate changes. A bond with duration of 8 years falls ~8% in price for every 1% rise in rates. Critical for fixed income portfolio construction.
ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)
A fund that tracks an index (e.g. S&P 500) and trades on an exchange like a stock. Low-cost, liquid, and transparent. AI is increasingly used in ETF screening, weighting, and rebalancing decisions.
Derivatives
Financial instruments whose value derives from an underlying asset — options, futures, swaps. Used for hedging risk or speculative positioning. AI tools for derivatives pricing are among the most technically complex in the space.
