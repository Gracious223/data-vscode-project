Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts and portfolio managers use in every investment conversation — the vocabulary that lets you challenge assumptions and ask the right product questions during discovery.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding — the total market value of a company. "Large cap" (£10B+), "mid cap" (£2–10B), "small cap" (<£2B). AI signals are often filtered and weighted by market cap — important to understand why.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price significantly. Highly liquid assets (US Treasuries, large-cap equities) trade instantly at scale. Illiquid assets can take days to exit. AI tools improving liquidity discovery are among the most valued buy-side applications.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares and selling them, expecting to buy back cheaper and pocket the difference. Short interest data (% of shares sold short) is a key sentiment signal — rising short interest often precedes intensive analyst scrutiny or negative news.
Basis Points (bps)
One hundredth of a percentage point. 100bps = 1%. The standard unit for yield, spread, and rate changes. When the Fed "raises rates by 25bps," it moves from 4.75% to 5.00%. Every analyst and PM you work with speaks in bps — learn it fluently.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
A company's net profit divided by shares outstanding — the primary metric analysts use to value a stock. "Beating EPS estimates" means the company earned more per share than expected. It is typically the single biggest catalyst for an immediate share price move.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E)
Share price ÷ EPS. A stock at 20× P/E means investors pay £20 for every £1 of annual earnings. High P/E signals growth expectations; low P/E may signal undervaluation — or a value trap. Analysts reference P/E multiples in virtually every valuation conversation.

EU AI Act · Europe

EU AI Act D-Day: High-Risk AI Obligations Now Enforceable — Finance Is at the Centre

From August 2, 2026, EU AI Act obligations for high-risk AI systems are enforceable. Credit scoring, fraud detection, and insurance underwriting AI fall under the high-risk classification. Maximum penalty: €15M or 3% of global annual turnover. A proposed Omnibus amendment would push the deadline to December 2027, but it has not been enacted — regulators advise treating August 2026 as operative.

Why It Matters

If your product influences credit, risk, or access-to-services decisions, it may already be a "high-risk AI system" under the Act. That status triggers mandatory human oversight, documentation, transparency, and accuracy requirements — which must be built in before deployment, not patched in during an audit.

Research & Think About
  • Read the EU AI Act's Annex III — map your product against the high-risk criteria; if it's ambiguous, that ambiguity is a product risk to flag now
  • Understand what "human oversight" requires under the Act specifically — it is more prescriptive than "a human reviews the output"
  • Read the Finextra piece on August 2026 compliance — the most practical current guide for financial services AI builders on what enforcement looks like

The IMF's new technical note on agentic AI in payments identifies governance, interoperability, and systemic risk concentration as the primary design questions for regulators and financial institutions.
