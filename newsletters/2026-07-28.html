Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six terms your portfolio managers use daily — and that shape how they evaluate every AI tool you build. With hyperscaler earnings this week and chip stocks selling off today, several of these are especially live right now.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell now, aiming to buy back cheaper later. Short sellers profit from falling prices. Today's chip selloff reflects short sellers who were already positioned for a decline in AI infrastructure stocks.
Yield Curve
A graph of bond yields across maturities (e.g. 2-year vs. 10-year). A normal curve slopes upward; an inverted curve — short-term yields above long-term — has historically predicted recessions. AI models are now used to nowcast curve shifts in real time.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
How expensive a stock is relative to its earnings. A high P/E signals expected growth. AI infrastructure stocks like Nvidia trade at elevated P/Es because the market prices in future AI revenue that has not materialised yet — which is exactly what is being tested this week.
Market Capitalisation
Total market value of all outstanding shares (price × shares). It determines index inclusion and weighting. When Nvidia falls 5% in a day, that moves the entire S&P 500 because of its index weight — roughly 6% of the index.
Earnings Season
Four annual windows (January, April, July, October) when most listed companies report quarterly results. Your users are busiest during these periods — AI products that earn deep adoption tend to prove themselves most visibly in earnings season.
Dark Pools
Private trading venues where institutional investors execute large block trades away from public exchanges to avoid moving the market. They represent roughly 40% of US equity volume. AI is increasingly used to determine optimal routing between dark pools and lit markets.
