Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users speak in the precise language of markets. Knowing these terms lets you ask better questions in discovery, write more credible PRDs, and earn the room with analysts and portfolio managers.

Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01%. Interest rates, spreads, and fees are always quoted in bps. A 25bps rate cut means rates fall 0.25%. Analysts talk in bps — you should too.
Credit Spread
The yield difference between a corporate bond and a government bond of the same maturity. Wider spreads signal more perceived risk. AI tools that surface spread movements give credit analysts a speed advantage.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them, then buying them back later at (hopefully) a lower price. Short sellers profit from price declines. AI is now used to detect short squeeze conditions and crowded short positions.
P/E Ratio
Price-to-Earnings ratio: what investors pay per £/$ of annual profit. A high P/E means growth expectations are baked in. Analysts compare P/E across peers and history to assess relative value.
Sharpe Ratio
Measures risk-adjusted return — return above the risk-free rate divided by volatility. A higher Sharpe means more return per unit of risk. Portfolio managers use it to compare strategies on a like-for-like basis.
Sector Rotation
Moving capital between sectors (e.g. tech to energy) as the economic cycle shifts. AI tools that identify early rotation signals — earnings revisions, fund flow data, macro indicators — are increasingly central to PM workflows.
