Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

When portfolio managers talk about beta, Sharpe ratios, and enterprise value, they're using precise tools — not jargon. Knowing what these mean helps you ask better product questions and design more useful workflows. Six terms to lock in today.

Beta
A measure of how much a stock moves relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market. Beta >1 = more volatile. Beta <1 = more stable. Portfolio managers use beta to manage overall market sensitivity — AI tools that calculate real-time beta are high-value.
Sharpe Ratio
Risk-adjusted return: how much return is generated per unit of risk taken. A ratio above 1 is generally good; above 2 is excellent. It's one of the most widely cited performance metrics — and one AI tools are increasingly used to optimise in real time.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a firm manages on behalf of clients. It's the primary size metric for asset managers. A firm with $500B AUM managing that entirely with human analysts is a very different product opportunity from one running agentic AI at scale.
Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01% (one-hundredth of one percent). 100bps = 1%. Used for interest rates, yields, and performance differences. When BCG says "300–500bps of P&L value," they mean 3–5 percentage points of margin — material at scale.
Market Cap vs. Enterprise Value
Market cap = share price × shares outstanding. Enterprise value (EV) = market cap + debt − cash. EV is preferred for M&A and valuation because it captures the full cost of acquiring a business. Analysts frequently use EV/EBITDA as a valuation multiple.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares and selling them, expecting the price to fall — then repurchasing at a lower price to return them and pocket the difference. Hedge funds use short selling to profit from declining stocks or to hedge long positions. AI sentiment tools are frequently deployed to identify short candidates.
