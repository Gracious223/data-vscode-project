Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Fixed income and derivatives vocabulary is central to the Goldman AI bond story above. Here are six terms that will help you read this market credibly alongside your analysts.

Beta
A measure of how much a security moves relative to the overall market. A beta of 1.5 means the asset tends to move 50% more than the index. AI risk models now compute real-time beta across thousands of securities.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a period. A "max drawdown" of 20% means the portfolio fell 20% from its highest point. AI scenario tools help PMs model drawdown risk under stress conditions.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes — expressed in years. A bond with duration of 5 falls ~5% in price for every 1% rise in rates. Critical for fixed income analysts managing rate risk.
Credit Default Swap (CDS)
A contract that functions like insurance against a bond issuer defaulting. If a company defaults, the CDS seller pays the buyer. Widely used by credit analysts to hedge or speculate on corporate default risk.
Total Return Swap (TRS)
A derivative where one party pays the total return of an asset (price gains + income) and receives a fixed or floating rate in return. Goldman used TRS in its new AI bond basket — letting investors gain exposure without owning bonds directly.
Tracking Error
How much a portfolio's returns deviate from its benchmark index. Low tracking error = closely mirrors the index. High = more active bets. AI factor models are used to optimise tracking error in quantitative strategies.
