Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six fresh terms today — all different from yesterday's edition. These appear constantly in the conversations portfolio managers and analysts have, and fluency with them signals you belong in the room.

Drawdown
Peak-to-trough loss in a portfolio over a period. "Maximum drawdown" is the worst case historically. AI products that alert portfolio managers on drawdown thresholds in real time are among the highest-value risk tools available.
Sharpe Ratio
Return divided by volatility. A Sharpe of 1.0 means earning 1% return per unit of risk. Higher is better. AI-powered factor strategies are routinely evaluated by their Sharpe improvement versus the benchmark.
Yield Curve
A plot of interest rates across different maturities (e.g. 2-year vs. 10-year government bonds). An inverted yield curve (short rate higher than long) has historically signalled recession. Macro analysts model yield curve shifts continuously.
Market Capitalisation
Share price × total shares outstanding. Defines size — large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap. Portfolio managers think in market cap tiers when constructing portfolios and selecting appropriate valuation multiples.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E)
Stock price divided by annual earnings per share. The most common equity valuation multiple. AI tools that screen for P/E anomalies against historical ranges are core to quantitative equity strategies at hedge funds and asset managers.
Book Value
A company's assets minus its liabilities. Price-to-Book (P/B) ratio compares market value to book value. AI models flagging P/B divergences from historical norms surface potential mispricing opportunities for equity analysts.
