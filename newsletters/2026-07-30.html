Lead Story

JPMorgan Builds AI Agents That Beat the 60/40 Portfolio in Backtests

JPMorgan researchers built a suite of portfolio-management AI agents using models from OpenAI and Anthropic that outperformed a traditional 60/40 stock-bond portfolio by 0.7 percentage points annually with lower volatility, across two decades of historical simulation. All eight agents tested also beat JPMorgan's own rules-based market-regime model. These are backtests — not live performance — and the bank explicitly cautions against reading them as proof AI can consistently beat markets.

Why It Matters

Your portfolio managers are asking the same question JPMorgan's researchers asked. The more important result is that AI agents beat JPMorgan's own rules-based model — meaning your product isn't competing with human analysts, it's competing with the firm's existing systematic processes.

Research & Think About
  • Learn what a "60/40 portfolio" assumes — and why it underperformed in the high-rate environment of 2022–24: that's the context your analysts bring to these results
  • Ask your data science team what it would take to replicate a multi-agent portfolio simulation on your firm's internal data
  • Read the JPMorgan backtesting paper — focus on what assumptions are baked in and where live performance would likely diverge

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's lead stories centre on portfolio construction, benchmarking, and the trust question around AI-managed capital. These six concepts sit at the heart of those debates — and will come up whenever you're talking to your users about what AI should and shouldn't be doing with their portfolios.

Backtesting
Testing an investment strategy on historical data to estimate how it would have performed. Backtests are evidence, not proof — live performance can diverge significantly when market conditions shift.
The 60/40 Portfolio
The classic balanced portfolio: 60% equities, 40% bonds. Long the default benchmark for moderate-risk investors, though it struggled badly in the high-rate, high-inflation environment of 2022–24.
Active vs. Passive Management
Active funds aim to beat a benchmark through stock selection or timing; passive funds track an index. AI-managed funds sit in active territory — trying to outperform, not just replicate.
Risk-Adjusted Return
Return measured relative to the risk taken to achieve it. The Sharpe Ratio is the most common metric: return minus risk-free rate, divided by volatility. Higher is better. AI agents beating 60/40 with lower volatility is a risk-adjusted win.
Market Regime
A distinct market environment with characteristic behaviour — bull run, bear market, high-volatility, low-rate. JPMorgan's existing rules-based system was specifically a "market-regime model." AI agents learning to identify regimes is a key research frontier.
Fiduciary Duty
The legal obligation to act solely in a client's best financial interest. When AI makes all the investment calls, the fiduciary question becomes: who is responsible if the AI causes harm? This is the core regulatory tension in AI-managed portfolios.

Finance Teams Are Racing to Deploy Agentic AI — Governance Infrastructure Is the Bottleneck

44% of finance teams now use agentic AI — up over 600% year-on-year. Lloyds identifies fraud investigation, credit support, compliance, and complaints processing as the primary deployment zones in 2026. The bottleneck is not model capability: it is governance infrastructure and auditability. Most firms have the ambition; few have the accountability framework to match it.

Why It Matters

The firms that win in agentic AI this year are not the ones with the best models. They are the ones with accountability frameworks — and that is a product design problem, not a technology problem. Building governance into your product from day one is how you earn deployment rights at regulated firms.

Research & Think About
  • Define what an accountability framework looks like in your product: who is named as accountable when an agent takes a wrong action — and is that person's name in your PRD?
  • Research the difference between deterministic agents (rules-based, predictable) and probabilistic agents (LLM-driven) — your compliance team needs to understand this distinction before any deployment sign-off

FCA · UK

FCA CEO Rathi: "Sandboxes, Not Prescriptive Rules" — Rethinking Regulation for the Age of AI

FCA CEO Nikhil Rathi told techUK's Agents of Change conference that AI regulation must be rethought from the ground up. The FCA will lean on regulatory sandboxes, active supervision, and cross-sector collaboration rather than writing new prescriptive AI rules. Agentic AI — AI that acts, not just assists — is explicitly flagged as the regulators' primary near-term concern.

Why It Matters

Rathi's position signals what the FCA will actually test. "We won't write rules for AI" means Consumer Duty and SM&CR apply in full — and the FCA will use supervisory visits to enforce them. Auditability and explainability in your product are not nice-to-haves; they're what a supervisor will look for.

Research & Think About
  • Read Rathi's full speech — focus specifically on his comments about agentic AI and what "step change" means in regulatory terms: it signals where supervision will intensify first
  • Understand the FCA's regulatory sandbox (Project Innovate) — could your product benefit from formal sandboxing to test AI outputs under regulatory supervision?
  • Ask your compliance team: is your AI product currently documented in a way that a supervisory firm visit would find acceptable under existing Consumer Duty obligations?

Product Architecture

Opus 5's Effort-Level Toggle — A Product Feature That Changes How You Design for Finance

Fortune's analysis of Opus 5 focuses on its defining product innovation: a five-level effort dial that lets users trade off intelligence for cost and latency within a single model. It is the first time Anthropic has made the cost-intelligence tradeoff an explicit product interface — shifting the architecture question from model selection to query-level optimisation.

Why It Matters

The effort toggle makes explicit what has always been implicit: not every analyst query deserves the same depth of reasoning. Your product architecture question is: which workflows need high-effort deep synthesis, and which need low-effort speed? Getting this wrong is expensive in both cost and analyst trust.

Research & Think About
  • Map your product's query types by required reasoning depth: simple data retrieval vs. document synthesis vs. multi-step scenario analysis — where does each sit on the effort dial?
  • Understand the cost-latency-quality tradeoff triangle — ask your engineering team how effort-level routing would work in your product's current architecture
  • Research how Bloomberg's ASKB and Vanguard's Expert Insights handle the speed-quality tradeoff in their UX — how do they signal to users what kind of output to expect?

The PM Role Is Bifurcating — Strategy and Business Acumen Are Now the Primary Differentiators

2026 survey data shows the PM role is splitting: strategic PMs focused on business outcomes versus technical PMs embedded in AI systems and data pipelines. 59% of product professionals rate strategy and business acumen as the most critical skill for the next 2-3 years — above execution, delivery, or technical depth.

Why It Matters

59% ranking strategy above execution confirms the direction your role should be moving. The risk is misreading this as PMs don't need technical depth. Strategy is what you do with technical comprehension, not instead of it — the PMs being squeezed out are the ones who lack both.

Research & Think About
  • Write a product strategy memo — not a roadmap — for your current product: one page, no feature lists, answering only "why this, why now, why us?" and "what would need to be true for this to fail?"
  • Research how Lenny Rachitsky defines strategic PM vs. execution PM — and self-assess honestly which you are spending most of your time being right now
  • Ask your manager: what would your PM role look like in 12 months if you deliberately leaned into strategy — and what proof points would get you there?
