Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Portfolio managers and analysts speak a precise language built around risk, return, and market structure. Six more terms you will encounter constantly — and need to own.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding. Large-cap stocks (above ~£10B) are typically more liquid and widely held. Small-cap stocks carry higher risk but may offer more alpha opportunities. Analysts segment the market by cap size constantly.
Beta
A measure of an asset's sensitivity to market movements. A beta of 1.0 means it moves with the index. Beta above 1 = more volatile than the market; below 1 = more stable. Portfolio managers manage beta exposure deliberately — it shapes the risk profile of the whole book.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes, measured in years. A bond with 7-year duration falls ~7% in price if rates rise 1%. Duration is how fixed income portfolio managers think about rate risk — it's the key number in any bond discussion.
Credit Spread
The yield premium a corporate bond pays over a risk-free government bond of the same maturity. Tight spreads mean investors are confident; wide spreads signal concern about default risk. Spread widening is often one of the first indicators of market stress.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
Total assets minus liabilities, divided by shares or units outstanding. The NAV per share is the "true value" of a fund at any given point. For ETFs, NAV underpins the arbitrage mechanism that keeps the trading price close to the underlying value of the portfolio.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a given period. A "maximum drawdown" tells you the worst loss an investor could have experienced. Risk-conscious portfolio managers track drawdowns obsessively — it's as important as return when evaluating a strategy.
