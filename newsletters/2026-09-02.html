Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

When hedge funds talk about "crowding," "dark pools," and "earnings season," these aren't abstractions — they're daily operating contexts for the analysts and portfolio managers your products serve. Six more terms to lock in.

Drawdown
The peak-to-trough percentage decline in a portfolio's value. Max drawdown is the worst loss sustained over a period. Analysts use it to assess downside risk. AI risk tools often surface drawdown risk before it crystallises.
Market Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without significantly moving its price. High liquidity = tight spreads, fast execution. Low liquidity = wider spreads, market impact. AI tools that predict liquidity shifts are highly valued by trading desks.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest price a seller will accept (ask). Wider spreads signal lower liquidity or higher uncertainty. It's a live indicator of market confidence and a core trading cost.
Correlation
How two assets move in relation to each other. A correlation of +1 means they move in lockstep; -1 means perfectly opposite; 0 means no relationship. Diversification only works if correlations are low — and AI is being used to detect correlation breakdowns in real time.
Dark Pools
Private, off-exchange trading venues used by institutions to execute large orders without revealing intent to the market. They account for roughly 15-20% of US equity volume. AI systems are increasingly used to route orders between lit and dark venues for best execution.
Earnings Season
The quarterly period (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct) when public companies report financial results. It generates the highest-volume analyst activity: earnings calls, estimate revisions, surprise reactions. AI summarisation and sentiment tools are in peak demand during earnings season.
