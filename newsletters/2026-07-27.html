Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six terms your portfolio managers use daily — and that shape how they'll evaluate every AI tool you build for them. Understanding these lets you ask better discovery questions and write sharper PRDs.

Drawdown
Peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a period. Max drawdown is a key risk metric. AI tools that flag approaching drawdown thresholds in real time are among the most requested features by risk teams.
Basis Points (bps)
One hundredth of one percentage point (0.01%). A 25bps rate cut means a 0.25% reduction. Used constantly for rates, spreads, and fee comparisons. When a PM says "we're 50bps over benchmark," they mean 0.5% ahead.
Beta
Measures how much a stock moves relative to the broader market. Beta > 1 = more volatile than the market; < 1 = less. AI can now estimate dynamic beta in near-real-time, shifting how risk desks monitor portfolios.
Active vs. Passive Management
Active managers pick stocks trying to beat the index. Passive managers track an index (e.g. S&P 500) at low cost. Your users are active managers — they need AI to help justify their fees against passive funds that are winning on cost.
Systematic vs. Discretionary
Systematic funds use rules-based, quantitative strategies (algorithms decide). Discretionary funds rely on human judgment. AI is blurring the line — your product likely sits at that intersection.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
Total assets minus liabilities divided by shares outstanding — the per-share value of a fund. For mutual funds and ETFs, it's the price at which investors buy or redeem shares. Reported daily after market close.
