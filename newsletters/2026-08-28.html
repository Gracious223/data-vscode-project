Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users — portfolio managers and analysts — operate in a world defined by these terms. Today's set covers the measurement vocabulary you'll encounter in investment conversations, earnings reviews, and fixed income discussions.

Market Capitalisation
Total market value of all outstanding shares (share price × shares). Nvidia crossed $5 trillion this week. The key metric for comparing company size and index weight.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
Share price divided by earnings per share. The most common equity valuation multiple. A high P/E implies investors expect strong future growth — common in AI-related stocks.
Yield Curve
A graph showing interest rates across different bond maturities. A "normal" curve slopes upward (longer = higher yield). An "inverted" curve — short rates above long rates — has historically preceded recessions.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes, measured in years. A bond with 5-year duration falls ~5% when rates rise 1%. Portfolio managers use duration to manage interest rate risk.
Basis Points (bps)
The standard unit for quoting interest rates and spreads. 1 basis point = 0.01%. "The Fed raised rates by 25bps" means rates rose by 0.25%. Essential for fixed income conversations.
Benchmark
A standard index (e.g. S&P 500, MSCI World) against which performance is measured. Portfolio managers are judged on whether they beat their benchmark — this is what alpha means in practice.

Research Spotlight

The Self-Driving Portfolio: 50-Agent AI Pipeline Challenges the Role of the Human Portfolio Manager

Andrew Ang (ex-BlackRock), Nazym Azimbayev, and Andrey Kim published "The Self-Driving Portfolio" — a 50-agent agentic architecture for institutional asset management. Specialised agents produce capital market assumptions, construct portfolios using 20+ competing methods, critique and vote on outputs, and a meta-agent rewrites its own code to improve future performance. The entire system is governed by the firm's Investment Policy Statement. CFA Institute published a detailed critique of its production-readiness.

Why It Matters

This isn't a thought experiment — it's a documented architecture by a former BlackRock chief investment strategist. The investor's role shifts from analytical execution to oversight and objective-setting. That is the workflow your product must be designed around.

SEC Makes AI Governance a Formal 2026 Examination Priority — Policies Must Now Be Enforced, Not Just Written

The SEC's Division of Examinations embedded AI oversight across its 2026 priorities, moving from principle to audit. Examiners are checking whether firms have adequate AI policies, whether those policies are actually enforced, and whether representations about AI capabilities are accurate. Robo-advisers, AI-driven analytics, and trading algorithms are all within scope.

Why It Matters

If your firm has an AI policy document but no enforcement mechanism, the SEC now considers that a finding. Governance is no longer a risk management exercise — it's an examination category your firm will be graded on. Your product's audit trail is part of that grade.

Global Regulation

AI Governance Is Now a Global Examination Priority — SEC, FCA, and ASIC All Move in Concert

ACA Group's August analysis documents a coordinated global shift: the SEC, FCA, and ASIC are all independently embedding AI governance into examination frameworks for 2026. Despite differing rule structures — the US uses existing antifraud law, the UK relies on SM&CR — the practical outcome for compliance teams is the same: AI outputs must be auditable and accountable to a named individual.

Why It Matters

Your firm likely operates across multiple jurisdictions. Building your product to the highest common denominator — EU AI Act + UK SM&CR + SEC antifraud — is now the only defensible architectural position. Compliance by coincidence won't survive a cross-jurisdictional examination.
