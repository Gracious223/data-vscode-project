Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily. Building this vocabulary is how you earn the room — and design products that solve the right problems.

Yield Curve
A graph showing bond yields across different maturities. When short-term yields exceed long-term ones ("inverted"), it historically signals recession. Analysts watch it daily as a macro signal.
Bid-Ask Spread
The difference between the highest price a buyer will pay and the lowest a seller will accept. A wide spread signals low liquidity — harder and more expensive to trade. AI systems optimise execution around this.
Beta
A stock's volatility relative to the broader market. Beta > 1 means it moves more than the market; < 1 means less. Portfolio managers build and hedge exposure to beta deliberately.
Equity Risk Premium
The excess return investors expect from equities over a risk-free rate (e.g. government bonds). It is the fundamental justification for holding stocks and a key input to valuation models.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
The total value of a fund's assets minus its liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. The benchmark price for mutual and hedge funds. AI tools that track NAV in real time are core wealth management infrastructure.
Market Maker
A firm that continuously quotes buy and sell prices, providing liquidity to markets. Banks and specialist firms act as market makers. AI now drives much of the quoting and inventory management at major market makers.
