Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Every term here connects directly to decisions your analysts and portfolio managers make daily. Knowing this vocabulary lets you ask better product questions — and earn credibility in the room.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares and selling them, expecting to buy them back cheaper later. Short sellers profit when prices fall. AI tools that surface negative signals faster are directly valuable here.
Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding. The most basic measure of a company's size. "Large-cap" firms (>$10B) behave very differently to "small-cap" — this affects how analysts use data and tools.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest a seller will accept (ask). A wide spread signals low liquidity — relevant when your product surfaces real-time market data.
Sharpe Ratio
Return earned per unit of risk taken. A Sharpe of 1.0+ is generally considered good. Portfolio managers use it to compare strategies — AI tools that improve it (not just returns) are genuinely valued.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a firm manages on behalf of clients. It's the primary scale metric for asset managers — and shapes how much they'll spend on tools and technology.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
The per-unit value of a fund's assets minus liabilities. Critical for mutual funds and ETFs. Analysts track NAV premiums and discounts to spot pricing anomalies and arbitrage opportunities.
